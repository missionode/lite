The Framework
The seven chakras
The whole programme is built on the chakra system: a traditional map from the yoga tradition of seven centres running from the base of the spine to the crown of the head. Each centre is a place in the body to rest attention on and a quality of the mind to explore. We climb one centre a day, Root to Crown, and then bring all seven together.

01
Day 01 · Breathe
Root Chakra · Muladhara
Grounding & Safety
Meditation basics, healthy expectations, breathwork, stability, and belonging.
▾
Practice focus

Breath awareness and Box Breathing. The foundation — using the breath as your first anchor of attention.

The Idea

Day 1 is about arriving. Most people walk in carrying the noise of their day — deadlines, notifications, half-finished conversations. The Root Chakra sits at the base of the spine and represents our most fundamental needs: safety, stability, belonging. Before the mind can go anywhere, the body needs to feel that it’s safe to be here.

We begin by setting healthy expectations. Meditation is not about emptying the mind — that’s a myth that stops more people than it helps. The mind thinks. That’s what it does. Meditation is the practice of noticing where attention goes and gently bringing it back. Every return is a repetition, the way a bicep curl is a repetition. The wandering is not failure — the noticing is the practice.

Points
Introduce the chakra system as the map for all nine days: seven centres from the base of the spine to the crown, each a place to rest attention and a quality to explore. We climb one centre a day, Root to Crown, then bring all seven together on Days 8 and 9. Present it as a traditional, symbolic map — useful because it gives attention somewhere to go, not something anyone has to believe in.
For the petals the orientation installs (Nyasa), use the petal chart as your reference: every petal’s letter, place and traditional quality.
Open with a question: “How many thoughts do you think you had in the last five minutes?” Research suggests the average is about 6,000 thoughts a day. We don’t control them — but we can learn to choose which ones we follow.
Explain the “security guard” metaphor for the Root Chakra — it’s the part of your nervous system that decides if the environment is safe enough for the rest of your mind to relax. If the guard isn’t satisfied, nothing else opens up.
Introduce Box Breathing with a live demonstration. Breathe in for 4 counts, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. The same technique used by Navy SEALs and first responders — it directly activates the parasympathetic nervous system. The audience practises together for 3 rounds.
Connect breath to grounding: when we slow the breath, the body’s threat-detection system stands down. Heart rate drops. Muscles soften. This is the physical foundation of what the tradition calls “finding your root.”
Close with the Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra: Verse 3 describes the space between breaths as the first doorway. This programme begins exactly there — at the pause between exhale and inhale, where the mind touches stillness for the first time.

Day 02 · Imagine
Sacral Chakra · Svadhisthana
Creativity & Emotion
Emotion, boundaries, visualization, and the introduction to Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra.
▾
Practice focus

Guided Visualization. Using the mind's natural image-making ability to build focus, calm, and directed awareness.

The Idea

The Sacral Chakra, just below the navel, governs creativity, emotion, and the ability to feel pleasure without being ruled by it. Day 2 moves from survival to sensation. Once the body feels safe (Day 1), it begins to notice — feelings, desires, creative impulses. The question shifts from “Am I safe?” to “What do I feel?”

We introduce Guided Visualization here because it speaks the Sacral Chakra’s language: imagery, colour, flow. The mind already thinks in pictures — visualization simply directs that natural ability. We also introduce the Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra, the ancient text that underpins the deeper techniques in this programme.

Points
Start by revisiting Day 1’s breath anchor. Ask: “Did anyone notice thoughts appearing between the counts?” Normalise it. That noticing is exactly the skill we’re building.
Explain the Sacral Chakra through water metaphors — emotions are like water. They flow. You can’t grip water, and you can’t stop emotions. But you can learn to sit by the river instead of being swept away by it. Healthy emotional boundaries are the riverbanks.
Guide a simple visualization: imagine a warm orange glow pooling just below the navel. With each breath, it expands gently. This isn’t fantasy — it’s directed attention using imagery, and the nervous system responds to vivid internal images almost as if they were real.
Introduce the Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra in accessible terms. It’s not religion; it’s a 5,000-year-old lab manual for consciousness. Where modern science asks “What is the brain doing?” the VBT asks “What happens when you look at where the looking comes from?”
Close with the practical note on emotional boundaries — creativity and emotional awareness are powerful only when you can also step back from them. That stepping back is what we’ll build tomorrow with focused attention.

Day 03 · Focus
Solar Plexus Chakra · Manipura
Confidence & Agency
Disciplined attention, kind approach to distraction, personal power.
▾
Practice focus

Focused Attention (Dharana). Sustained concentration on a single point — training the mind to stay where you place it.

The Idea

The Solar Plexus, above the navel, is the seat of will, confidence, and personal agency. Day 3 introduces Focused Attention (Dharana) — the practice of holding the mind on a single point. If breath is the anchor and visualization is the canvas, focused attention is the pencil that draws a deliberate line.

Distraction is not the enemy. The kind approach to distraction is one of the most important ideas in this programme. When the mind wanders, the habit is to judge yourself — “I’m bad at this.” The discipline is to notice, release the judgement, and return. Every return is the actual practice.

Points
Open with a demonstration: place a single point of focus — a sound, a candle flame, or the sensation at the tip of the nose. Ask the group to hold attention there for 60 seconds. Then discuss what happened. The answers always reveal the same truth: the mind left, and they brought it back.
Explain Dharana as the “mental gym.” Just as physical exercise needs resistance (gravity, weight), mental training needs resistance too (distraction, boredom, discomfort). The Solar Plexus represents the fire of will — the ability to choose where your energy goes. Every time you bring attention back, you’re doing a rep.
The kind approach to distraction: share the analogy of training a puppy. You don’t shout at a puppy for wandering — you gently pick it up and set it back down. Your attention is the puppy. Self-criticism doesn’t improve focus; it makes the mind noisier. Kindness is the faster path.
VBT connection: Verse 26 describes gazing at a single point until awareness dissolves into the point itself. That’s the deeper possibility of focused attention — the observer and the observed merge. We’re not aiming for that today, but it’s where this road leads.
Wrap: by Day 3 participants have three tools — breath, imagery, and focus. That’s a complete starter kit. The next days add depth — observation, compassion, expression, and connection.

Day 04 · Notice
Heart Chakra · Anahata
Compassion & Awareness
Emotional awareness, body scan, gratitude, healthy boundaries.
▾
Practice focus

Body Scan, Noting and Labeling. Observing sensations, thoughts and feelings without reacting or following them.

The Idea

The Heart Chakra is the midpoint — the bridge between the body-oriented lower centres and the mind-oriented upper centres. It governs compassion, gratitude, and the ability to hold emotional pain without becoming it. Day 4 introduces the Notice module — Body Scan and Noting — because real compassion starts with noticing what’s actually happening inside you, without story or judgement.

Most of us don’t notice our bodies until something hurts. Body Scan reverses that habit — it trains you to pay attention to the whole field of sensation, gently, region by region. A sensation in the knee is just a sensation — until the mind adds “I’m getting old.” Noticing the sensation before the story arrives is the skill we build here.

Points
Begin with a brief recap: “You’ve learned to ground the body (Root), connect with feeling (Sacral), and direct the mind (Solar Plexus). Now we move into the territory of the heart — where the practice becomes personal.”
Explain Anahata: the word means “unstruck sound” — a vibration that exists without being hit, without cause. The heart centre represents a kind of awareness that doesn’t need a reason to be compassionate. It just is. That’s different from empathy, which is reactive. Compassion is a choice.
Guide a Body Scan from the crown of the head to the soles of the feet. Move slowly. At each region, the instruction is: “Notice what’s there. Don’t change it. Don’t name it if you can help it. Just feel it.” When you find tension, the instruction is the same: notice. Don’t fix. Fixing is the Solar Plexus habit. Here, we’re developing the observer.
Introduce Noting and Labeling with a live exercise. Sit in silence for 2 minutes. Every time you notice the mind doing something, silently label it. “Thinking.” “Hearing.” “Itching.” “Planning.” The label is soft, not judicial. It’s a whisper, not a verdict. This creates a gap between you and the activity of your mind.
Close with gratitude as the heart’s natural language. One practical technique: name three specific things from today — not categories (“family”) but specific moments (“my daughter’s laugh when the dog stole her toast”). Specificity is what makes gratitude feel real instead of routine.

Day 05 · Release
Throat Chakra · Vishuddha
Expression & Letting Go
Ho’oponopono, Undo & Unlearn, authentic expression, the value of silence.
▾
Practice focus

Ho'oponopono, Undo & Unlearn. Letting go of patterns, resentment, and mental habits that no longer serve you.

The Idea

The Throat Chakra governs expression — not just speaking, but authentic communication. And authentic expression begins with releasing what blocks it. Day 5 introduces Ho’oponopono and Undo & Unlearn — practices that clear the emotional and mental debris sitting between what you feel and what you actually say.

Ho’oponopono is the Hawaiian practice of reconciliation built on four phrases: “I’m sorry. Please forgive me. Thank you. I love you.” Deceptively simple, it works on a deep principle: you don’t need to understand a wound to release it. Undo & Unlearn goes further — recognising habitual patterns of thought and choosing not to follow them. Not suppressing, but seeing clearly and deciding freely.

Points
Open with a listening exercise: two minutes of complete silence. Not meditation — just listening. To the room, to the sounds outside, to the sound of your own breathing. Then ask: “What did you hear that you hadn’t noticed before?” The point: we hear more when we stop preparing what to say next.
Introduce Ho’oponopono through a story: “Dr. Ihaleakala Hew Len used this practice while reviewing files of patients in a psychiatric ward — without meeting them. He took responsibility for what he found in himself while reading their stories. The ward transformed. Whether you believe the story literally or not, the principle is real: when you change your inner relationship to pain, your outer relationships shift.”
Guided practice: participants silently repeat the four phrases while directing attention to someone they have tension with. Emphasise that “I love you” in this context is not romantic; it’s recognition. You’re recognising the shared humanity in the other person.
Introduce Undo & Unlearn: “Think of a response you give automatically. Someone criticises your work, and you defend. Someone offers a compliment, and you deflect. Those are patterns — learned, not chosen. The practice is to notice the pattern in the moment before you follow it, and then choose. You might still defend. But now it’s a choice, not a reflex.”
VBT connection: Verse 38 speaks of focusing on the space inside the throat — the void from which sound emerges. The tradition recognised that the seat of expression is also the seat of silence. The most powerful communicators are the ones who are also comfortable in silence.

Day 06 · Integrate
Third Eye Chakra · Ajna
Observation & Intuition
Body awareness, clear seeing, the seven-chakra framework begins, interpretation without reaction.
▾
Practice focus

Seven-chakra guidance, Resonant Energy Meditation, and mindful music. Weaving everything into a complete practice.

The Idea

The Third Eye, between the eyebrows, represents insight — the capacity to observe without being caught by what you observe. Day 6 introduces the Integrate module, where the seven-chakra guided framework begins. All five modules learned so far are brought into a continuous upward journey — Root to Third Eye — and for the first time, participants experience what a full integrated practice feels like.

Integration is not a new technique; it’s the skill of weaving what you already know into a whole. The Third Eye Chakra is the natural home for this — it’s the centre of clear attention, where you see the pattern across all the individual parts. A good integrated practice is more than the sum of its pieces.

Points
Begin with the distinction between observation and interpretation. Hold up an object (a pen, a glass). Ask: “What is this?” People will say its name, its use, its colour. None of those are the object itself — they’re labels. The Third Eye practice is seeing the thing before the label.
Explain integration: “Over five days you’ve built five skills — breathing, imagining, focusing, noticing, releasing. Today we string them together. Think of it like learning chords on a guitar, and now playing your first song. The chords haven’t changed. But together they make something new.”
Guide the first integrated chakra journey: start at Root with breath, move to Sacral with visualization, Solar Plexus with focused attention, Heart with body awareness, Throat with silent release, Third Eye with open observation. Each transition is a gentle shift, not a hard switch. The whole thing flows like one continuous meditation.
VBT connection: multiple verses (15, 28, 29) describe meditation on the body’s inner space — feeling the skin as a boundary, then sensing the awareness that pervades the whole body equally. That’s exactly what a good integrated practice approaches: the shift from scanning individual parts to feeling the body as one field of sensation.
Close with intuition: “Intuition is pattern recognition below the threshold of conscious reasoning — your brain has noticed something your thinking mind hasn’t caught up with yet. When you train integration, you lower that threshold. You begin to notice more, earlier. That’s not supernatural; it’s a trained skill.”

07
Crown Chakra · Sahasrara
Connection & Spacious Awareness
Meaning, humility, the complete seven-chakra framework, open awareness.
▾
The Idea

The Crown Chakra represents the point where individual awareness meets something larger — however you define that. For some, it’s spiritual. For others, it’s the sense of being part of a system bigger than yourself — nature, community, the sheer improbability of being alive. Day 7 is the summit of the chakra climb. All seven centres are activated in sequence for the first time.

No new module today. Instead, we deepen. Spacious awareness — the practice of opening attention to everything at once without grabbing any of it — is what the Crown invites. Up to now, we’ve been narrowing attention. Today, we reverse. Open the lens. Let everything in.

Points
Open with the journey recap: “Seven days. You started at the ground — am I safe? — and moved through feeling, will, compassion, expression, and observation. Now: can you hold all of it at once? Can you feel the whole field of your awareness without zooming into any one part?”
Explain spacious awareness: “Up to now, we’ve been focusing attention — narrowing it to a breath, an image, a sensation. Today, we reverse. Don’t focus on anything. Let everything in — sounds, sensations, thoughts — and just watch the whole panorama. This is what the Crown represents: awareness that doesn’t need an object.”
Guide the full seven-chakra meditation for the first time. Root to Crown, all seven, using every module learned. This is the complete practice. It should take 20–25 minutes. The transition from Third Eye to Crown is the quietest: just let go. Stop directing. Rest in what remains.
VBT connection: Verse 110 describes the state where there is no object of meditation — just awareness aware of itself. That’s the Crown state. We’re not claiming anyone will arrive there in one evening, but the direction matters. Every practice so far has been preparing the ground.
Close with humility: “The Crown Chakra is traditionally depicted with a thousand petals — meaning infinite openness. The humility is in recognising that you’ll never finish. There’s always more to notice, more to release, more to integrate. And that’s not a failure — it’s the point.”

Integration
Integrated Sound Experience
All seven chakras together, sound-therapy-inspired meditation, personal choice of add-ons.
▾
The Idea

Day 8 is where the modules converge with sound. Instead of learning something new, participants revisit the full seven-chakra journey accompanied by sound-therapy-inspired meditation. Singing bowls, tuned drones, and nature textures support each chakra stage. The sound isn’t the practice; it’s the environment the practice moves through.

Participants also choose two add-on techniques from the modules they’ve experienced — the ones that resonated most. This is the beginning of personalisation: the programme gives you a toolkit, and you start choosing which tools you’ll carry forward. Comfortable volume and personal choice are emphasised.

Points
Open by framing Day 8 as the “rehearsal for real life.” In the programme, each day has a structure. In real life, you won’t always have that. Day 8 asks: which practices stuck? Which ones felt natural? That’s your personal meditation language.
Explain the science of sound: “Sound waves are physical vibrations. When a singing bowl resonates at a low frequency, your body responds — muscles relax, breathing slows. This isn’t metaphysical; it’s entrainment. Your nervous system syncs with consistent rhythmic input. It’s the same reason a heartbeat calms a newborn.”
VBT connection: Verses 41–42 describe Nada meditation — focusing on an internal, unstruck sound. The tradition recognised that deep silence isn’t the absence of sound; it’s the awareness of a subtler vibration underneath. Sound meditation is a bridge — you ride an external sound inward until you begin to hear the silence between the notes.
Guide the group through a condensed seven-chakra sound journey. Root: deep drum or bass bowl. Sacral: flowing water textures. Solar Plexus: fire-crackling overtones. Heart: warm mid-range singing bowl. Throat: high crystal bowl or chime. Third Eye: binaural-style drone. Crown: open silence or a single fading tone.
Close with the personal choice element: “Of the six modules — Breathe, Imagine, Focus, Notice, Release, Integrate — which two felt most like home? Those are your add-ons tonight. Tomorrow, we build your personal plan around them.”

Completion
Resonant Energy Meditation
Uplifting rhythmic sound, mantra-inspired energy, mindful music, personal continuation plan.
▾
The Idea

Day 9 is the culmination. Resonant Energy Meditation combines everything — breath, visualization, focused attention, body awareness, observation, release, and the full chakra framework — into one continuous, uplifting experience. Rhythmic sound, mantra-inspired vocal energy, and mindful music create a shared field of practice. This isn’t a quiet sit — it’s alive, energised, and communal.

But the most important part of Day 9 is what happens after the meditation: building a personal plan. The programme ends. The practice doesn’t have to. Each participant identifies their strongest anchor, their preferred technique, and a realistic rhythm they can commit to.

Points
Open with energy: “For eight days, we’ve been mostly still. Today, we move. Not physically — energetically. Resonant Energy Meditation uses rhythmic breathing, vocal toning, and music to build an ascending wave through all seven centres. Think of it as the difference between a lake and a river — today, we let the energy flow.”
Guide the Resonant Energy session: start with deep, rhythmic breathing. Add humming or toning (a comfortable “om” or “hum” sound — no specific mantra required, personal choice respected). Layer in music — rhythmic, ascending, designed to build energy. The group moves through each chakra with increasing intensity, then softens to silence for the Crown.
VBT connection: Verse 73 describes the experience of being filled with ecstatic energy — where the boundaries between self and experience dissolve, not through withdrawal but through fullness. When every centre is vibrating, when breath and voice and attention are all aligned, there are moments where the separation between “me meditating” and “the meditation” thins.
Transition to the personal plan: “This programme gave you a map. Now you choose the trail. Three questions: (1) Which single technique would you do if you only had 5 minutes? That’s your anchor. (2) Which deeper practice do you want to keep exploring? That’s your growth edge. (3) When — realistically — will you practise? Don’t choose the ideal. Choose the real.”
Close the programme: “You leave with three things. A choice of attention anchors — breath, image, sensation, sound. A kinder way to return when the mind wanders. And a mind that’s already different from the one that walked in nine days ago. Not because something was added, but because you learned to pay attention to what was already there.”
