# Nine-Day Meditation Curriculum

## Purpose

This nine-day programme introduces meditation gradually through breath awareness, the seven-chakra contemplative framework, focused-attention practices, visualization, body awareness, sound-based meditation, reflection, and integration. It is designed for novice meditators and can be delivered by a trained facilitator using the Chakra Meditation application.

The curriculum treats chakras, mantras, frequencies, and related imagery as traditional contemplative frameworks—not medical diagnoses or guaranteed treatments. Participants should remain free to modify posture, keep their eyes open, reduce sound, skip a practice, or stop at any time.

## Programme outcomes

By the end of Day 9, participants should be able to:

- Describe meditation in practical, beginner-friendly language.
- Use breath, bodily sensation, sound, and attention as meditation anchors.
- Describe the traditional themes and body locations associated with the seven chakras.
- Recognise thoughts, emotions, and sensations without immediately reacting to them.
- Choose preparation and integration practices appropriate to their present capacity.
- Build a realistic personal meditation routine.
- Distinguish spiritual or symbolic teachings from medical and psychological treatment.
- Practise with autonomy, self-compassion, responsibility, and respect for personal boundaries.

## Facilitator principles

- Invite rather than command: use phrases such as “if comfortable” and “you may.”
- Never promise healing, awakening, special powers, success, or permanent emotional change.
- Do not ask participants to disclose trauma, private intentions, confessions, or memories.
- Do not interpret physical sensations as proof of blocked or opened chakras.
- Keep sound at a comfortable level and warn participants before bells, mantras, or strong tones.
- Encourage anyone experiencing pain, panic, dizziness, dissociation, or distress to stop, orient to the room, and seek appropriate support.
- Preserve confidentiality while explaining that confidentiality cannot cover an immediate risk of harm.
- Provide water, breaks, accessible seating, and an eyes-open alternative.
- End every deep practice with grounding and orientation before discussion.

## Suggested daily rhythm

Every day follows a recognisable structure:

1. Arrival and wellbeing check
2. Review of the previous day
3. Teaching and discussion
4. Demonstration or short exercise
5. Break
6. Main guided session
7. Grounding and quiet reflection
8. Group discussion and questions
9. Home practice

## Day 1 — Foundations and Root Chakra

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Guided session:** Box Breathing + Root Chakra

### Learning objectives

- Understand what meditation is and is not.
- Establish expectations, consent, posture, and safety.
- Experience breath as a practical attention anchor.
- Understand the chakra system as a traditional symbolic map.
- Explore the traditional themes of the Root Chakra.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Welcome, introductions, accessibility needs, and group agreements |
| 0:20–0:45 | What meditation is: attention, awareness, repetition, and choice |
| 0:45–1:05 | Expectations, common misconceptions, and questions |
| 1:05–1:25 | Breath mechanics and comfortable breathing practice |
| 1:25–1:35 | Break |
| 1:35–2:00 | Introduction to the seven-chakra framework |
| 2:00–2:20 | Root Chakra teaching and grounding exercise |
| 2:20–2:45 | Guided Box Breathing + Root Chakra session |
| 2:45–3:00 | Reflection, questions, and home practice |

### Interesting discussion content

#### What meditation really trains

Discuss meditation as the repeated act of noticing where attention has gone and choosing how to respond. A “successful” session is not necessarily silent, blissful, or thought-free. Returning after distraction is the training.

#### Expectations versus experience

Invite discussion around common expectations: instant calm, an empty mind, unusual visions, perfect posture, or immediate life changes. Contrast these with realistic outcomes such as recognising tension sooner, pausing before reacting, and developing consistency.

#### The breath as messenger and anchor

Explore how breathing changes with movement, emotion, speech, and rest. The goal is not to force a “correct” breath but to notice it and use a comfortable pattern when appropriate. Explain that anyone who feels light-headed should return to natural breathing.

#### Understanding chakras responsibly

Introduce *chakra* as a Sanskrit term commonly translated as “wheel.” Explain the seven-chakra model as one influential later yogic framework among several traditions. The app uses it as an organised path for attention, imagery, reflection, mantra, and personal meaning—not as anatomical proof or diagnosis.

#### Root Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Muladhara
- Contemplative location: base of the spine/pelvic floor region
- Symbolic themes: safety, stability, belonging, embodiment, material foundations
- Reflective question: “What helps me feel supported and present?”

Avoid claiming that fear, financial problems, or physical symptoms prove a Root Chakra imbalance.

### Experiential activities

- Compare feet-on-floor, seated, and standing grounding.
- Practise noticing five points of physical support.
- Demonstrate Box Breathing gently and allow natural breathing as an alternative.

### Home practice

- Five minutes of natural breath awareness.
- Record one observation: what helped attention return?
- No requirement to evaluate whether the Root Chakra felt “open.”

## Day 2 — Sacral Chakra and the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra

**Duration:** 2 hours
**Guided session:** Box Breathing → Guided Visualization → Root + Sacral Chakras

### Learning objectives

- Review grounding and breath regulation.
- Explore the Sacral Chakra through creativity, emotion, pleasure, adaptability, and boundaries.
- Receive a careful introduction to the *Vijnana Bhairava Tantra*.
- Learn how visualization differs from fantasy, prediction, and guaranteed manifestation.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Arrival and Day 1 review |
| 0:15–0:35 | Sacral Chakra teaching |
| 0:35–1:00 | Introduction to the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra |
| 1:00–1:15 | Visualization: purpose, consent, and common difficulties |
| 1:15–1:25 | Break |
| 1:25–1:50 | Guided session |
| 1:50–2:00 | Grounding, discussion, and home practice |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Sacral Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Svadhisthana
- Contemplative location: lower abdomen/pelvic region
- Symbolic themes: creativity, feeling, pleasure, adaptability, relationship, healthy boundaries
- Reflective question: “How can enjoyment and responsibility coexist?”

Discuss the difference between wholesome pleasure and compulsive escape. Enjoyment need not produce guilt, and restraint need not become suppression.

#### Introducing the Vijnana Bhairava Tantra

Present it as a classical nondual Shaiva contemplative text framed as a dialogue between Bhairava and Bhairavi. It is known for many concise contemplative approaches involving breath, sensory experience, space, sound, attention, and transitions. Avoid presenting every modern meditation technique as a direct quotation from it.

Useful discussion themes:

- Why traditions offer multiple meditation doorways
- How one person may connect with breath while another connects with sound or space
- The value of transitions: the pause after exhalation, the moment a sound ends, or the space between thoughts
- Technique as a doorway rather than a performance

#### Visualization without pressure

Visualization may be vivid, vague, sensory, verbal, or almost absent. Participants do not need to “see” a cinematic image. The purpose is to become familiar with an internally represented experience while retaining practical judgement and realistic action.

### Home practice

- Five minutes of breath awareness followed by three minutes of gentle imagery.
- Reflect privately on one ordinary activity that supports creativity or healthy enjoyment.

## Day 3 — Solar Plexus Chakra and Focused Attention

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Guided Visualization → Focused Attention (Dharana) → Root + Sacral + Solar Plexus Chakras

### Learning objectives

- Understand focused attention as a trainable skill.
- Explore agency, disciplined action, confidence, and realistic effort.
- Distinguish confidence from control, superiority, or perfectionism.
- Practise returning repeatedly to one anchor.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival, review, and short breath practice |
| 0:20–0:50 | Solar Plexus Chakra teaching |
| 0:50–1:20 | Attention, distraction, and Dharana |
| 1:20–1:35 | Demonstration: visual anchor and returning |
| 1:35–1:45 | Break |
| 1:45–2:25 | Guided session |
| 2:25–2:50 | Discussion: effort without force |
| 2:50–3:00 | Home practice |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Solar Plexus Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Manipura
- Contemplative location: upper abdomen/navel region
- Symbolic themes: agency, confidence, discipline, digestion of experience, purposeful action
- Reflective question: “What is one action within my genuine control?”

#### The attention cycle

Teach a simple cycle:

`Choose an anchor → notice distraction → release judgement → return`

The return is not evidence of failure; it is the repetition that develops concentration.

#### Deep work and humane discipline

Discuss focused work as a bounded period of attention supported by rest, preparation, and realistic expectations. Concentration is not proof of intelligence or spiritual advancement. Fatigue, stress, environment, and health all influence attention.

### Experiential activities

- One-minute focus on a shape or colour.
- Repeat with environmental sounds present.
- Compare forceful concentration with relaxed steadiness.

### Home practice

- One five-minute Dharana practice.
- Choose one manageable task and work on it without switching for ten minutes.

## Day 4 — Heart Chakra and Ho’oponopono

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Focused Attention → Root + Sacral + Solar Plexus + Heart Chakras → Ho’oponopono

### Learning objectives

- Explore compassion with boundaries.
- Understand the difference between empathy, agreement, forgiveness, reconciliation, and trust.
- Learn the app’s optional Ho’oponopono-inspired reflective practice.
- Practise warmth without forced emotional disclosure.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival and concentration review |
| 0:20–0:50 | Heart Chakra teaching |
| 0:50–1:20 | Compassion, boundaries, and relational safety |
| 1:20–1:40 | Ho’oponopono context and safeguards |
| 1:40–1:50 | Break |
| 1:50–2:35 | Guided session |
| 2:35–3:00 | Grounding, discussion, and journaling |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Heart Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Anahata
- Contemplative location: centre of the chest
- Symbolic themes: care, grief, connection, compassion, balance, boundaries
- Reflective question: “Can care and a clear boundary exist together?”

#### Compassion is not self-erasure

Discuss why kindness does not require agreement, access, reconciliation, or accepting mistreatment. Healthy compassion includes care for oneself and respect for another person’s autonomy.

#### Ho’oponopono-inspired reflection

Explain that the app uses an optional practice inspired by familiar contemporary Ho’oponopono phrases. Participants need not recall a person, relive an event, forgive before they are ready, or contact anyone. The phrases can be heard simply as sounds or general intentions.

### Home practice

- Five minutes of compassionate breathing.
- Notice one moment when kindness and a boundary can coexist.

## Day 5 — Throat Chakra and Undo & Unlearn

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Root + Sacral + Solar Plexus + Heart + Throat Chakras → Ho’oponopono → Undo & Unlearn

### Learning objectives

- Explore communication, listening, truthfulness, and restraint.
- Practise honest acknowledgement without disclosure or self-attack.
- Understand the three distinct forgiveness possibilities.
- Experience content-free release without memory search or forced analysis.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival and Heart Chakra review |
| 0:20–0:50 | Throat Chakra teaching |
| 0:50–1:20 | Listening, speaking, silence, and boundaries |
| 1:20–1:45 | Undo & Unlearn framework and safeguards |
| 1:45–1:55 | Break |
| 1:55–2:40 | Guided session |
| 2:40–3:00 | Quiet integration and questions |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Throat Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Vishuddha
- Contemplative location: throat/neck region
- Symbolic themes: expression, listening, truthfulness, timing, discernment
- Reflective question: “What supports communication that is both honest and kind?”

#### Speech, silence, and responsible expression

Discuss how communication includes speaking, writing, listening, asking for time, and choosing silence. Silence can be mindful, but it can also become avoidance; speech can be courageous, but it can also become impulsive.

#### Undo & Unlearn: content-free practice

Participants are not asked to remember, identify, explain, confess, speak, type, or mentally answer anything. The practice offers permission for learned responses to soften without claiming that memory or behaviour has been erased.

Clarify three separate possibilities:

1. Forgiving oneself without removing responsibility
2. Privately praying for forgiveness from another person without imagining their answer or contacting them
3. Forgiving another person only if and when it feels safe

Forgiveness does not automatically mean approval, reconciliation, restored trust, renewed access, or removal of consequences.

### Home practice

- Practise one minute of listening before responding in a safe conversation.
- Write one sentence about responsible communication without documenting private session content.

## Day 6 — Third Eye Chakra and Body Scan

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Body Scan → Root + Sacral + Solar Plexus + Heart + Throat + Third Eye Chakras → Undo & Unlearn

### Learning objectives

- Develop interoceptive awareness without diagnosing sensations.
- Explore discernment, imagination, concentration, and critical thinking.
- Distinguish intuition from certainty.
- Practise observing sensations without immediately changing them.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival and communication review |
| 0:20–0:50 | Third Eye Chakra teaching |
| 0:50–1:20 | Interoception, interpretation, and uncertainty |
| 1:20–1:40 | Body Scan demonstration |
| 1:40–1:50 | Break |
| 1:50–2:40 | Guided session |
| 2:40–3:00 | Discussion and grounding |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Third Eye Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Ajna
- Contemplative location: forehead/between the eyebrows
- Symbolic themes: discernment, insight, imagination, attention, reflective judgement
- Reflective question: “How can inner impressions and reliable information work together?”

#### Sensation versus interpretation

Help participants distinguish:

- Direct sensation: pressure, warmth, movement, tingling
- Interpretation: “This means something is wrong”
- Story: “This always happens because…”

The Body Scan practises remaining with direct sensation while allowing uncertainty.

#### Intuition and evidence

Intuition can invite attention, but it does not remove uncertainty. Encourage comparing impressions with evidence, experience, professional knowledge, and trusted advice where appropriate.

### Home practice

- Ten-minute head-to-toe Body Scan.
- Record sensations using neutral language without diagnosing them.

## Day 7 — Crown Chakra and Guided Noting

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Body Scan → Guided Noting → all seven chakras, Root through Crown

### Learning objectives

- Complete the seven-chakra progression.
- Learn neutral Noting/Labeling.
- Explore meaning, connection, humility, and uncertainty.
- Practise releasing labels after using them.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival and Body Scan review |
| 0:20–0:50 | Crown Chakra teaching |
| 0:50–1:20 | Guided Noting: purpose and method |
| 1:20–1:40 | Short Noting exercise and questions |
| 1:40–1:50 | Break |
| 1:50–2:40 | Guided full-chakra session |
| 2:40–3:00 | Integration and home practice |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Crown Chakra themes

- Traditional name: Sahasrara
- Contemplative location: top of the head/above the crown
- Symbolic themes: meaning, connection, perspective, humility, transcendence
- Reflective question: “What gives life meaning without requiring absolute certainty?”

#### Guided Noting

Noting uses a brief neutral mental label and then releases it. The purpose is not to classify everything perfectly or stop thoughts. It creates a small space between an experience and an automatic reaction.

Discuss:

- Why the exact label is less important than noticing
- How repeated thoughts can be labelled repeatedly without frustration
- Why labels should remain neutral rather than “good” or “bad”
- When to stop labelling and return to grounding

#### Connection with humility

Explore how a sense of connection can coexist with uncertainty, cultural respect, practical responsibility, and openness to learning.

### Home practice

- Five minutes of Guided Noting followed by two minutes without labels.
- Review which meditation anchor has been most accessible so far.

## Day 8 — Integrated Practice and Sound Therapy

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** Facilitator-approved choice of any two compatible add-ons + all seven chakras

### Learning objectives

- Integrate techniques without overloading the session.
- Understand the roles of music, mantra, drone, bell, silence, and spatial sound.
- Practise selecting techniques according to need rather than novelty.
- Develop facilitator awareness of volume, consent, and transitions.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival and technique review |
| 0:20–0:50 | Sound, attention, expectation, and safety |
| 0:50–1:15 | App sound architecture and facilitator controls |
| 1:15–1:35 | Group chooses two compatible add-ons |
| 1:35–1:45 | Break |
| 1:45–2:35 | Integrated sound-supported full-chakra practice |
| 2:35–3:00 | Listening review and facilitation discussion |

### Interesting discussion content

#### What sound can and cannot do

Sound can provide an anchor, shape atmosphere, mark transitions, support rhythm, and influence subjective experience. Do not claim that a specific frequency cures disease, repairs organs, or produces a guaranteed emotional outcome.

#### Elements of the app’s soundscape

- Narration: orientation and guidance
- Background music: continuity and atmosphere
- Mantra: repetition and symbolic focus
- Drone/tone: sustained attention anchor
- Bell/bowl: transition marker
- Silence: integration rather than absence
- Spatial processing: perceived placement and depth, not a therapeutic guarantee

#### Designing a balanced session

Discuss why more layers are not always better. Consider participant sensitivity, speaker versus headphone playback, room acoustics, volume, duration, and the cognitive load of too many techniques.

### Practical workshop

Have participants compare short samples with:

- Narration plus music
- Narration plus silence
- Mantra with and without spatial processing
- A deliberate fade versus an abrupt stop

Keep comparisons short and at comfortable levels.

### Home practice

- Choose one simple sound-supported practice and one silent practice.
- Note which context each practice serves best.

## Day 9 — Consolidation, HRIM Session, and Music Jam

**Duration:** 2–3 hours
**Guided session:** High Energy HRIM session + structured music jam

### Learning objectives

- Review and integrate the complete programme.
- Experience HRIM as the app’s high-energy contemplative mode.
- Create a sustainable personal practice plan.
- Use collaborative sound with clear boundaries and listening discipline.
- Close the group process safely and meaningfully.

### Suggested agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Arrival and nine-day review |
| 0:25–0:50 | What changed in understanding, not promised outcomes |
| 0:50–1:10 | HRIM context, intensity, and sound safety |
| 1:10–1:40 | High Energy HRIM session |
| 1:40–1:50 | Break and grounding |
| 1:50–2:25 | Facilitated music jam |
| 2:25–2:45 | Personal practice planning |
| 2:45–3:00 | Closing circle and programme feedback |

### Interesting discussion content

#### Reviewing progress without comparison

Invite participants to reflect on skills rather than dramatic experiences:

- Can I notice distraction sooner?
- Can I return without harsh judgement?
- Which anchors feel accessible?
- Can I recognise when to stop or modify a practice?
- Can I distinguish symbolism from factual or medical claims?

Participation in discussion should remain optional.

#### HRIM practice

Present HRIM as a traditional seed-mantra-inspired high-energy focus within the app. Explain that “high energy” describes the intended character of the session, not a guaranteed physiological result or proof of spiritual attainment. Offer lower volume, shorter duration, natural breathing, or observation-only alternatives.

#### Structured music jam

Suggested rules:

- Begin with one minute of listening.
- Introduce one sound at a time.
- Leave space between sounds.
- Keep volume below the agreed limit.
- Use a visible signal for quieter, pause, and stop.
- No participant must sing, chant, or play.
- End gradually, followed by silence and orientation.

Possible non-prescriptive sound sources include voice tones, humming, simple percussion, bells, bowls, clapping, or available instruments.

#### Building a sustainable personal practice

Each participant creates a simple plan:

- Preferred practice and anchor
- Realistic duration
- Likely time and place
- Minimum version for difficult days
- Signs that rest or external support is needed
- How progress will be reviewed without perfectionism

### Closing reflection

Invite, without requiring, each participant to share:

- One skill they are taking forward
- One boundary they will respect
- One practice they want to continue

End with grounding, appreciation, and clear orientation back to ordinary activity.

## Facilitator assessment and completion

Completion should be based on participation and understanding, not unusual meditation experiences. Suggested evidence includes:

- Attendance and respectful participation
- Ability to explain basic meditation safety
- Ability to distinguish observation from interpretation
- Ability to choose and modify an anchor
- Understanding that chakra and sound frameworks are contemplative rather than diagnostic
- A realistic personal practice plan

Do not rank participants by perceived chakra activation, depth, visions, stillness, emotional disclosure, or ability to tolerate long sessions.

## Materials checklist

- Chakra Meditation PWA and tested playback device
- Speakers suitable for the room; headphones only by individual choice
- Reliable power and offline-ready assets where possible
- Chairs, mats, cushions, and accessible seating
- Drinking water
- Timer visible to the facilitator
- Printed programme and safety information
- Optional private reflection sheets
- Feedback forms
- Clear emergency and participant-support procedure

## Daily facilitator record

After each day, record only operational information:

- Attendance
- Timing and breaks
- Techniques delivered
- Technical or audio issues
- Accessibility adjustments
- General questions requiring follow-up
- Changes recommended for the next day

Do not record private meditation content, inferred diagnoses, confessions, or sensitive personal disclosures unless an authorised safeguarding procedure specifically requires documentation.
